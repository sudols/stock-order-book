# design thinking and innovation project milestone 1 bca

## product title

open source multi symbol stock order book simulator for indian markets

## describe your product

this project proposes to build an open source stock order book and trading simulator that mirrors how indian equity and derivatives markets process orders and match trades across multiple symbols in real time, the core idea is to design a matching engine that accepts common order types like market, limit and possibly stop orders and then maintains a time and price prioritized order book for each listed symbol while generating trades whenever compatible buy and sell orders cross, around this core engine we plan to add a simple but clear user interface where students, retail investors, fintech enthusiasts and startups can connect, submit simulated orders, see live depth of market and replay the full history of trades to understand how liquidity forms and disappears around different price levels in an indian context, the system will start with equities and then gradually allow configuration for futures and options style contracts where lot sizes, tick sizes and trading hours follow indian exchange rules in a simplified way, the project will be released under an mit license so that colleges, small brokers or startups can host the engine locally or on cloud and change the matching logic, user interface or analytics pipeline without seeking permission from a vendor, while the initial deployment would run with delayed or synthetic data the architecture will be kept ready so that in future versions it can be connected to live or end of day data feeds from indian exchanges or data vendors subject to compliance and availability, finally we will document the engine design and publish apis so others can plug in their own dashboards, risk modules or algo trading experiments on top of the same open matching core.[cite:1]

## why the team has chosen this product

we chose this product because the working of a stock order book is central to any capital market yet most of the software that actually runs indian exchanges is proprietary and closed so students and small teams rarely get to see how such systems behave under different loads or in unusual market conditions, as bca students with interest in software engineering data structures and basic finance we felt this project sits at a natural intersection of our skills and learning goals and pushes us a little beyond ordinary course assignments, the matching engine requires careful thinking around performance, fairness of matching rules, concurrency and fault handling so it gives us scope to apply what we learned in algorithms and operating systems courses in a concrete setting, at the same time thinking about order types risk checks and regulatory constraints encourages us to read about sebi guidelines and indian exchange practices which increases our awareness of how technology shapes the broader economy, another reason is that indian markets have seen growth in retail participation and algo trading but educational tools that explain microstructure in an accessible and local way are still not very common, building an mit licensed engine and simulator makes it easier for other colleges and even school level programs to adapt the system without facing license fees or vendor lock in, we believe that even if the project remains a simulator and never becomes production grade it will still stay useful as a teaching and experimentation tool that we and others can keep refining over time.

## what problems it solves

indian stock exchanges today primarily run on closed source matching engines that are not available for academic study or small scale experimentation so students and early stage startups mostly interact with the markets through broker apis without understanding how the order book itself behaves underneath, this creates a gap between theoretical learning of order matching and real algorithmic thinking because learners cannot easily observe how volumes at best bid and ask change when many orders are submitted or cancelled together, our project addresses this by offering an open and inspectable order book implementation where every change to the book can be logged visualized and replayed and where the matching rules are fully documented, another problem is that commercial trading simulators or paper trading platforms that support indian markets are usually tied to specific brokers or proprietary frontends which limits how much one can customize the backend logic or integrate with custom analytics, with an mit licensed engine local teams can change data structures, add new metrics such as order to trade ratio, latency of matching and order book imbalance, and then study how different algorithms or strategies would have behaved in specific indian market scenarios such as budget day or large ipo listings, finally there is a broader policy level issue that much of critical market infrastructure knowledge stays concentrated with a few large technology vendors and exchanges so by publishing an open reference implementation we lower the entry barrier for research groups civic technologists or regulators who want to prototype new market designs or test stress scenarios without touching production systems.

## potential users and target audience

our primary users are indian college students in computer applications, engineering, mba and finance programs who need a practical tool to understand how order driven markets work and how software design choices affect fairness and performance, faculty members who teach courses on financial markets data structures or distributed systems can also use the simulator to create lab exercises where students implement new matching policies or order types and then compare results, another group of users are fintech startups and small brokerages that want a sandbox to test user interface flows risk checks or client order routing policies before they connect to real exchanges, because the engine is open source and can be deployed on modest hardware it can serve as a safe environment for these teams to experiment without affecting live customers, we also see use cases for retail traders and investor communities who want to learn about liquidity, slippage and impact cost using controlled experiments on indian style order books rather than generic global examples, finally policy researchers and think tanks studying indian capital market microstructure could run scenario based simulations to see how changes like new lot sizes or intraday margin rules might affect order flow although for such studies they would probably extend and validate our model further.

## innovations in this project

one planned innovation is to design the core matching engine as a set of modular components so that users can choose between different data structures for the order book such as simple sorted lists for teaching purposes or more efficient tree or heap based implementations for performance experiments and then compare how these choices affect throughput and latency under indian trading patterns, a second innovation is to support multiple symbols from the beginning of the design so that the same engine instance can maintain separate books for several equities or contracts while allowing cross symbol analytics like sector wide liquidity heat maps or correlation of order flow, this moves the simulator closer to how a small exchange or multi asset venue in india would operate rather than limiting it to a single instrument, third we plan to expose a well documented api that allows external clients to connect over http or websockets and submit orders, stream market depth and receive trade confirmations which encourages integration with custom dashboards educational mobile apps or algorithmic strategy backtesting tools that are focused on indian markets, another innovative angle is the emphasis on transparency and reproducibility where every matching decision along with time stamps and order attributes can be exported for analysis so learners can verify that price time priority and other rules are correctly implemented and can also test alternative matching policies such as frequent batch auctions in an indian context, finally by releasing the full stack under an mit license and writing deployment guides for low cost infrastructure we try to innovate not only in technology but also in accessibility since most existing matching engines with indian support are licensed products that smaller institutions rarely get to control.

## how it will differ from existing solutions

there are several commercial and semi commercial solutions that touch indian markets but they mostly work as closed simulators or broker specific paper trading platforms, for instance some indian brokers provide paper trading accounts where users can place virtual orders on live prices yet the backend remains a black box and the user cannot see the full depth of book or change the matching rules, global market simulators that are used in universities often focus on us or european markets with order types tick sizes and regulations that do not fully match indian conditions so while they are helpful conceptually they do not reflect local constraints such as specific intraday margin norms or auction mechanisms, our project differentiates itself by focusing specifically on indian style order books and by offering an open and modifiable engine instead of a fixed service, another dimension of difference is that we aim to support multiple symbols out of the box so that a class or startup can simulate trading across a small universe of equities or contracts instead of being limited to a single demonstration instrument, in terms of benchmarking we plan to compare our simulator against at least three categories of tools broker provided paper trading, international academic market simulators and generic charting platforms that offer simulated orders and show that while those tools are useful for practicing strategies they rarely allow inspection of the internal matching logic or adaptation of the engine, in contrast our system makes the engine itself the main artefact that can be forked, improved and audited by the community while still providing a basic but usable interface for day to day simulation.

## why it is challenging yet feasible in four months

building even a simplified stock order book that is correct under many edge cases requires careful design of order life cycle events such as placement, partial fills, cancellations and modifications so that alone introduces challenge in terms of logic and testing effort within the four month window, adding multi symbol support and a usable interface for submissions and visualizations increases complexity because we have to think about concurrency, data persistence and clear visual representation of depth and trades without overwhelming users, at the same time we believe the scope is manageable because we can prioritize a well tested core matching engine and a minimal though functional interface first and treat advanced analytics or complex order types as stretch goals, the technology stack can remain reasonably simple using mainstream languages and frameworks that the team already knows which keeps the learning curve under control, since the project is a simulator and not a production exchange we can also relax certain constraints such as ultra low latency or strict regulatory compliance which makes the engineering challenge ambitious but still suitable for one semester if we plan milestones carefully.

## how success and quality will be measured

we will measure success through a mix of technical functional and user centric indicators so that the evaluation reflects both correctness of the engine and usefulness for learners, on the technical side key parameters will include accuracy of matching against a well defined set of test cases covering various order types and sequences, stability of the engine under higher simulated order rates and ability to support multiple symbols without data corruption or performance collapse, we will track metrics such as maximum orders processed per second in a local test environment and average response time for order acknowledgement within the simulator, on the functional side we will check that core features promised in the scope are working including placing and cancelling orders, viewing level wise order book, replaying a session and exporting data for analysis, finally from the user perspective we plan to run small pilots with classmates or interested faculty where they use the tool for basic experiments and then share feedback through short surveys focusing on clarity of interface, ease of understanding order book dynamics and perceived usefulness for teaching indian markets, if by the end of the semester we have an engine that passes most tests, supports several symbols, is documented enough for others to run and receives reasonably positive feedback from initial users we will consider the first milestone successful with room for later refinement.

## resources required and feasibility

the main resources needed are development machines with standard programming tools, a code hosting platform like github for collaboration, and optionally a small cloud instance or local server to host the simulator for demonstrations, on the software side we plan to use open source languages and libraries so there will be no additional license cost which fits well with the goal of keeping the whole stack transparent and affordable in the indian academic context, we may need access to historical or near real time indian market data for testing order flows and constructing realistic scenarios however for the milestone we can begin with synthetic datasets created according to typical volume and volatility patterns described in public reports and move to more detailed data later if feasible, we will also depend on documentation from indian exchanges and sebi guidelines to ensure that our simplified rules are still aligned with real market structures even if they are not legally complete, overall the resource requirements look moderate and feasible within a student budget which makes the project practical to execute while leaving scope for future teams or partners to invest in better hardware and richer datasets.

## team responsibilities and assessment

if we work as a team of three or four members responsibilities will be divided clearly by name and enrolment so that each person has both an individual contribution and some shared overlap, one member will focus mainly on core matching engine design and implementation taking responsibility for order data structures event handling and unit tests that verify correctness, another member will own the api and integration layer that exposes endpoints or sockets for order submission and data streaming as well as basic authentication and logging, a third member will concentrate on the user interface including depth of market views trade history charts and configuration screens paying attention to usability for indian students and traders, if there is a fourth member they can specialize in data and analytics building modules that compute and visualize microstructure metrics such as spread, order book imbalance and volume profiles for indian symbols, assessment of each member will be based on code commits, documentation, test coverage and participation in design discussions rather than only the final demo, this way individual accountability remains visible even though the final product is integrated.

## planning short term and long term

in the short term over the first four to six weeks we plan to finalize requirements, study reference material on order books in indian markets, choose the tech stack and implement a basic single symbol matching engine with tests, in parallel we will sketch the api contracts and simple command line or web interfaces for placing orders and viewing the book so that by mid semester we can run end to end demos even if features are limited, in the next phase we will extend the engine to handle multiple symbols, refine performance and introduce logging and replay mechanisms so sessions can be stored and analysed later, towards the end of the four months focus will shift to polishing the interface, writing documentation, packaging the project for open source release and testing it with small user groups, in the longer term beyond this semester we see scope to add more realistic indian market features such as pre open auctions, circuit breakers, basic risk checks and support for different asset classes as well as connecting to external data feeds or broker apis for hybrid simulations, these longer term goals provide a roadmap for future contributors while keeping the first milestone focused on a solid core.

## supporting evidence and literature

there is growing literature on how market microstructure and order driven trading affect liquidity price discovery and volatility in emerging markets including india, academic papers and sebi or exchange reports often emphasize the importance of transparent and fair matching rules, depth of book information and proper risk management to support healthy participation of retail and institutional investors, at the same time many of these documents assume the existence of robust matching engines without offering accessible models for students or small teams to study and prototype, research on open source trading systems is more limited but there are examples of matching engine projects and simulators built for educational or experimental purposes in other countries and asset classes which show that it is possible to capture essential order book behaviour with modest resources, in india the rapid growth of algorithmic trading, discount brokerages and retail investing platforms has increased the need for better education around how orders travel from front end apps to exchanges and how they interact in the book, by grounding our project in this context we believe an open and inspectable indian style order book can contribute to capacity building, skills development and experimentation, even if adoption remains modest the presence of a local, mit licensed reference implementation gives educators and innovators a concrete artefact to extend rather than starting from scratch, we will compile a list of relevant indian and international studies, regulatory documents and open source projects as part of our documentation so that users can see how our design choices relate to broader research and practice.

## risk analysis and mitigation

there are several risks that could affect either the success of the project or our ability to finish it within the planned timeline, on the technical side we might underestimate the complexity of handling all edge cases in order management especially when multi symbol support and concurrency are added which could lead to bugs or inconsistent states in the book, to mitigate this we plan to start with a clear and minimal specification of order life cycle and build comprehensive unit and integration tests early in the project, another risk is scope creep where we are tempted to add too many advanced features like complex order types, sophisticated analytics or live data integration before the core engine is stable, we intend to control this by classifying features into must have, should have and nice to have and regularly reviewing progress with faculty, there is also a team risk related to uneven workload or dependency on a single member for critical modules, for this we will maintain shared documentation and code reviews so that knowledge is distributed and we can cover for each other if needed, finally there is an adoption risk where despite completing the simulator few people use it in practice, to address this we will engage potential users such as classmates and faculty early, gather feedback and adjust the interface and documentation so that the barrier to trying the tool remains low.

## stakeholder discussions and feedback

we plan to discuss the project with at least three types of stakeholders to refine our assumptions and design, from the industry side we would like to talk to someone working in a brokerage or fintech firm who has experience with indian exchange connectivity and can comment on practical order flow patterns and common pitfalls in designing trading systems, their reaction can help us avoid unrealistic assumptions and highlight missing features in our simulator, we will also speak with our parents or family members who invest in indian markets at a retail level to understand what confuses them about order placement and trade execution so we can think of ways to make the simulator more intuitive for non technical users, a third group will be teachers or academic mentors in finance or computer science who can advise on how such a tool could be integrated into coursework and what kind of interfaces or assignments would be most useful, by capturing and reflecting on the suggestions from these conversations during the milestone we can adjust our priorities and also demonstrate that the project idea has been exposed to diverse perspectives rather than being designed in isolation.

## ethics privacy moral and legal issues

because the project deals with financial market concepts and potentially with historical or live data there are several ethical and legal considerations even though it is a simulator, we need to ensure that users understand clearly that this is not a real trading platform and that no actual orders will reach indian exchanges so there is no promise of profit or investment advice, if we ever use anonymized or public market data we must respect the terms of use of data vendors and exchanges and avoid redistributing data in ways that violate licensing, from a privacy perspective if we collect user information such as login ids or usage logs for educational analytics we should store it securely, minimize personal details and be transparent about what is recorded, morally we should avoid positioning the simulator as a tool for encouraging highly speculative behaviour instead we can frame it as a learning environment to understand risks and market mechanics, legally if in future the project moves closer to live trading integration we would need to consider sebi and exchange rules around connectivity, testing environments and investor protection, for the scope of this semester we will keep the system purely educational and open source with clear disclaimers and will document any third party components or data sources used so that others who adopt the project in india can review compliance in their own context.
